ThreatRAG: An Adaptive Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation Framework for Explainable Cybersecurity Threat Intelligence and Incident Response

Ahsan Habib Sitab
School of Computing and Artificial Intelligence • MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning
AI506 Course Project: Multi-Agent RAG Systems • Email: sitab.ai.research@gmail.com • Repository: https://github.com/ahsitab/ThreatRAG
Abstract—Contemporary Security Operations Centers (SOCs) face escalating alert volumes, opaque machine learning anomaly detections, and severe hallucination risks when employing Large Language Models (LLMs) for threat triage. In this work, inspired by recent advances in multi-agent filtering (MAIN-RAG, ACL 2025), we propose ThreatRAG, an adaptive hybrid retrieval-augmented generation framework designed specifically for explainable cybersecurity threat intelligence and incident response. ThreatRAG coordinates four specialized agents: a Coordinator/Planner, a Cybersecurity Retrieval Specialist, a Security Reasoning Specialist, and an Evidence Verification Specialist equipped with an iterative feedback loop. The framework introduces a tri-modal adaptive hybrid retrieval engine combining dense semantic search (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), sparse lexical search (BM25Okapi), and structured network flow telemetry matching via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (k=60) and cross-encoder reranking. Furthermore, ThreatRAG addresses uncalibrated model overconfidence through post-hoc Platt scaling on a LightGBM anomaly detector, reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 0.0037 down to 0.0005 on 6,000 held-out flows. Evaluated on a curated 50-question benchmark covering threat identification, historical incident matching, evidence explanation, and defensive response, ThreatRAG achieves 100.0% citation precision and a 207% relative increase in answer correctness over a conventional single-agent RAG baseline. All defensive recommendations enforce a mandatory Human-in-the-Loop authorization gate to prevent automated service disruption.

Keywords—Multi-Agent Systems, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Cybersecurity, MITRE ATT&CK, Confidence Calibration, Explainable AI.

I. Introduction and Objectives

Modern cyber defense relies heavily on Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems and network intrusion detection pipelines. However, security analysts face crippling alert fatigue, often reviewing hundreds of anomalous events daily. While deep learning and gradient-boosted trees detect statistical anomalies in network packet flows, they operate as black boxes, providing no semantic explanation or actionable mitigation guidance.

Conversely, off-the-shelf Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate remarkable synthesis abilities but suffer from factual hallucinations, invented CVE identifiers, and confident errors in security contexts. Direct generation without verified grounding can mislead incident responders into taking counterproductive or disruptive containment actions.

To bridge this critical divide, we introduce ThreatRAG, a multi-agent RAG system built to achieve four core objectives:

  1. Autonomous Multi-Agent Triage: Decompose threat triage into specialized, collaborative roles: task planning, hybrid retrieval, calibrated reasoning, and evidence verification.
  2. Tri-Modal Adaptive Hybrid Retrieval: Unify dense semantic vectors, sparse lexical BM25 matching, and structured NetFlow statistical profiles.
  3. Probabilistic Confidence Calibration: Ensure predicted anomaly probabilities reflect empirical correctness via post-hoc Platt scaling.
  4. Verifiable Factual Grounding: Enforce bracketed citation auditing, epistemic separation between empirical observations and hypotheses, and an iterative feedback loop for insufficient evidence.

II. Selected Base Paper and Related Work

A. Selected Base Paper: MAIN-RAG (ACL 2025)

ThreatRAG is directly inspired by MAIN-RAG: Multi-Agent Filtering Retrieval-Augmented Generation, published by Chang et al. in ACL 2025 [1]. MAIN-RAG demonstrates that monolithic, single-agent RAG architectures fail in noisy environments because the generator must simultaneously evaluate context relevance and formulate an answer. MAIN-RAG proposes decoupling query planning, passage retrieval, filtering, and verification into specialized agents with an iterative feedback loop.

B. Adopted, Adapted, and Extended Concepts

While MAIN-RAG validated multi-agent filtering on open-domain question answering benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA), high-stakes cybersecurity presents unique domain challenges:

  • Adopted: We adopted MAIN-RAG's core architectural principle of decoupling verification from generation and utilizing an iterative feedback loop when retrieved evidence is insufficient.
  • Adapted: We adapted the filtering and verification logic to enforce adherence to established cybersecurity taxonomies, specifically MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise Matrix v14.1 and the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog.
  • Extended: We extended MAIN-RAG by introducing Tri-Modal Hybrid Retrieval (fusing text embeddings with structured network flow telemetry via Reciprocal Rank Fusion) and Post-Hoc Confidence Calibration (Platt scaling) to quantify epistemic uncertainty.

C. Other Related Work

Xiao et al. introduced MASS-RAG [2], focusing on multi-agent synthesis across conflicting documents. Es et al. developed RAGAs [3], providing automated metrics for RAG faithfulness. Lekssays et al. explored TechniqueRAG [4] for ATT&CK mapping. ThreatRAG synthesizes these concepts into a cohesive defensive system.

III. Proposed Multi-Agent Architecture

A. Agent Responsibilities and Coordination

ThreatRAG orchestrates four distinct logical agents as depicted in Fig. 1:

  • Agent 1: Coordinator / Planner: Parses the user query and incoming flow metrics, formulates a structured execution plan, schedules specialists, manages the shared workflow state (ThreatRAGState), and caps feedback retries (max_retries = 2).
  • Agent 2: Cybersecurity Retrieval Specialist: Queries persistent ChromaDB and BM25 indexes, handles structured flow matching, and executes query expansion upon feedback requests.
  • Agent 3: Security Reasoning Specialist: Executes the calibrated LightGBM anomaly classifier, maps behavior to MITRE ATT&CK techniques, separates empirical telemetry facts from hypotheses, and formulates prioritized playbooks.
  • Agent 4: Evidence Verification Specialist: Audits bracketed citations against retrieved chunk IDs, computes faithfulness scores, detects insufficient evidence, and triggers re-retrieval when claims lack support.

B. Mathematical Formulations

1) Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF): To unify rankings across dense embeddings, BM25 scores, and structured flow distance without scale distortion:

RRF(d) = ∑ [ w_m / (k + r_m(d)) ] for m ∈ {dense, sparse, structured}, k = 60

2) Expected Calibration Error (ECE): To assess the reliability of probability outputs:

ECE = ∑ (|B_b| / N) · |acc(B_b) − conf(B_b)| over B = 10 bins

Platt scaling fits a learned logistic sigmoid P(Y=1|z) = [1 + exp(-(Az + B))]-1 on the validation split.

IV. Knowledge Corpus and Datasets

ThreatRAG ingests authentic, publicly accessible security knowledge: (1) MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise STIX 2.1 (718 techniques), (2) CISA KEV Catalog (1,790 CVE records), (3) Curated Historical Incident Archive (Dyn DNS, SolarWinds, MOVEit, Target, WannaCry), and (4) CIC-IDS2017 Benchmark (30,000 balanced flows). In total, 1,074 structured knowledge chunks are indexed into persistent ChromaDB and BM25Okapi.

D. Qualitative Analysis: Successes and Failure Case

Success Case 1 (TID-001): Port 80 SYN flood (85k pkts/s). Baseline gave generic text advice; ThreatRAG correctly flagged DDoS with 99.98% Platt confidence and mapped T1498.001 with verified citations.

Failure / Insufficient Evidence Case (TID-014): Out-of-domain prompt ("quantum tachyon modulation"). Baseline hallucinated theoretical quantum exploits; ThreatRAG Verification Agent caught low similarity (< 0.15), triggered 1 feedback retry, and correctly abstained with an explicit uncertainty qualification.

VI. Limitations and Threats to Validity

  1. Latency Overhead: Multi-agent deliberation introduces 3.53s of latency, acceptable for Tier-1 SOC triage but requiring caching for high-speed automated inline filtering.
  2. Corpus Coverage: Evaluated on DDoS, reconnaissance, and landmark breaches; continuous ingestion is necessary for emerging zero-day vulnerabilities.

VII. Conclusion and Deliverables

ThreatRAG demonstrates that decoupling retrieval, reasoning, and verification into a collaborative multi-agent architecture resolves the critical hallucination and overconfidence challenges of standard RAG. With 100% citation precision, +207% correctness improvement, and ECE reduced to 0.0005, ThreatRAG offers a robust blueprint for explainable cybersecurity AI.
